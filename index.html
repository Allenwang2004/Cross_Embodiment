Summary

What this is

Meta Motivo is a behavior foundation model trained only on an adult body. A 256-dimensional latent z selects what it does. We freeze the whole policy and change only z, and ask whether it can drive a child's body (lengths × 0.611, 38 kg against the adult's 72 kg).

We first align the interface between body and policy. The actuators get PD gains and torque limits scaled to the body. The observation is converted by exact reverse retargeting into the equivalent adult observation. The policy's actions are joint target angles, which do not depend on body size, so they need no conversion. To the policy, it is still looking at an adult, one with weaker muscles.

With the interface aligned, the adult's own z0 is already close to usable on the child. Latent search closes the rest, and it is cheap: the corrections concentrate in an 8-dimensional subspace shared across motions, and starting straight from z0, 5 generations (80 rollouts) recover about 70% of the gain of a full search. Corrections for different motions are nearly orthogonal, and a learned, general adapter does almost nothing on unseen motions, so we search each motion quickly instead.

From the start

What each step changes, and by how much

Step 0 · baseline

Change nothing

Only the body is shrunk to child proportions. The actuators keep the adult settings (PD gains kp 180, kd 2, torque limit ±360 N·m), and the policy reads the child's own observation. Median L_align 1.82, mean joint-angle error 15.6°, and the body ends 2.0 m away from where the reference does.

Step 1 · actuators

Scale the actuators to the body

Adult torques are too strong for a 38 kg body. We scale the PD gains and torque limits to the body: kp 180 → 35.4, kd 2 → 0.31, ±360 → ±70.7 N·m. L_align drops to 1.53 and the drift from 2.0 m to 0.96 m, but the joint-angle error does not improve (17.5°), because the observation the policy sees is still wrong.

Step 2 · observation

Convert the child's state into an adult observation

The policy has only seen adult observations. The child's pelvis height, body positions and velocities are about 60% of the adult's, so the policy sees an adult state that makes no sense. A fixed per-feature multiplier (2–15% error) brings L_align to 1.08. Exact reverse retargeting copies the joint angles, divides root position and linear velocity by 0.611, keeps the ground clearance, and computes the adult observation with humenv's own formula. L_align drops to 0.62 and the joint-angle error halves (8.2°).

Step 3 · latent

Search 5 generations in an 8-dimensional subspace

What remains is the real dynamics difference: mass distribution, torque limits, inertia. That part goes to z. Starting from z0, 5 generations (80 rollouts) bring the median L_align to 0.43.

To the policy, it is controlling an adult with weaker muscles

With the observation in adult units, the policy sees an adult whose posture and velocities make sense. Its actions are PD target joint angles, and a given angle means the same thing on an adult or a child, so actions need no conversion. The only real difference left is dynamics: for the same target angle, the child's joints get there with different speed and force. No conversion can remove that, so z makes up for it.

Fast search

A few generations are enough

The search is an antithetic evolution strategy (ES) with Adam. Each generation draws 8 random directions and tries each with both signs (16 rollouts), estimates a downhill direction from the ranking of the results, and updates z. The cost is L_align.

z = 16 · normalize( z0 + U c ),   c ∈ ℝ⁸

U holds 8 correction directions: the top 8 principal components of the corrections z* − z0 found by full searches on 429 other motions. None of the 12 test motions is used to compute U. The search starts directly from z0, with no pre-search.

System

Two loops, and only c changes

Why not one general adapter

Corrections do not carry across motions

The obvious approach is a network that takes a motion's z0 and outputs the child's z. We tried supervised learning, an ES-trained adapter, and a closed-loop adapter that re-chooses z every second. On unseen motions the cost only drops to 0.85–0.99 of z0's. Searching the motion itself for 5 generations brings median L_align to 0.76 of z0's, and a full search to 0.40.

The best corrections for different motions are nearly orthogonal (cosine similarity about 0.03–0.06), and a single motion has many equally good solutions, so there is no consistent rule to learn. We spend the effort on making each motion's search fast instead of predicting its answer.

Limitations

What is not verified yet

  1. Small sample. The fast-search numbers come from 12 motions and one random seed; the 60-motion version has not finished.
  2. One body. Only the child. Other bodies need their actuators and observation conversion regenerated by the same pipeline.
  3. The reference is not the true optimum. The full search ran 2,048 rollouts with an anchor penalty toward z0, which is why some fast searches exceed 100%.
  4. Timings include parallel load. Time per generation was measured with many searches running at once. Each search also spends about a minute loading the model and building environments; one process handling motions back to back avoids that.
